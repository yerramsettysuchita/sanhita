The problem

Two compliance problems. One broken bridge.

The regulation exists as text a person reads. Compliance systems need rules a machine can run. Everything difficult about both problems comes from that one gap.

When the regulation changes

  1. SEBI publishes or amends a circular
  2. Which requirements actually changed?
  3. Which of our obligations do they touch?
  4. Which processes and controls are affected?
  5. What must this firm now do differently?

Today this is manual legal reading, circular by circular. The result is uneven implementation and delayed adaptation across firms in the same position.

Keeping up with what we already owe

  1. These are the duties this firm has
  2. What evidence proves we discharged each one?
  3. Did we actually discharge it, and on time?
  4. Where is the gap, and who owns fixing it?
  5. Did the fix work, and can we prove it?

Operationally intensive, largely manual, and hardest for smaller intermediaries with the least compliance resource.

The product

Your compliance lifecycle, in one place

Nine steps, in the order they happen. Each one produces something the next one needs, and every one of them is written down.

  1. 01

    Company

    Tell Sanhita who you are. Intermediary type, registration, the processes you run and the systems you run them on.

  2. 02

    Framework

    Declare which SEBI rulebooks govern you. Sanhita will not decide this for you; it is a legal judgement with consequences.

  3. 03

    Evidence

    Upload your own records. Reports, filing registers, dispatch logs, acknowledgements. PDF, CSV, XLSX and JSON are all read.

  4. 04

    Review

    Your margin report carries no rule id, so a person confirms which requirement each record satisfies. Nothing reaches the engine first.

  5. 05

    Assessment

    Run the certified rules against those records. The run is recorded with the hash of the rulebook and the hash of the evidence it used.

  6. 06

    Gap

    Exactly what is missing, late, or has no record at all. Each finding cites its clause, its page and the officer who signed the rule.

  7. 07

    Remediation

    An owner, a team, a deadline and a corrective action. There is no button anywhere that marks a gap fixed.

  8. 08

    Re-check

    Attach the corrected record to the task and run the same certified rule again. It closes only if the engine agrees.

  9. 09

    Audit

    The whole decision chain, hash chained. Altering one entry breaks every hash that follows it.

One requirement, end to end

From one SEBI sentence to one proven outcome

A real clause from the Master Circular for Stock Brokers, followed all the way to closure. This is the same path the application walks, and a test drives every step of it through the live routes.

  1. SEBI clause 40.1.8, page 95

    As like in derivatives segments, the TMs/CMs shall report to the Stock Exchange on T+5 day the actual short collection or non collection of all margins from clients.

  2. Compiled into a typed rule

    Stock broker must report short collection of margins, within 5 days of the trade date, evidenced by a report filing.

    Signing was blocked until an officer settled whether T+5 counts working days or calendar days. The clause does not say, so the compiler refused to decide.

  3. The firm's own records

    ABC Securities uploads its margin register. A person confirms which requirement each row satisfies.

  4. Assessment

    The certified rule runs against those records. No model is consulted, so the same evidence always produces the same answer.

  5. Gap

    The January filing was never made. The finding names clause 40.1.8, page 95, and the officer who signed the rule.

  6. Remediation

    Owned by Operations, due in seven days, with the corrective action recorded against it.

  7. Corrected evidence

    The firm files the return and attaches that record to the task. Re-checking is refused until it does.

  8. Closed

    The same certified rule runs again and returns no finding. Nobody declared this fixed. The engine did, and the whole chain verifies.

The other journey

When SEBI changes the rule, know what changes for you

Replayed on a genuine amendment, not on an edit we applied by hand. SEBI issued a Master Circular for Investment Advisers in June 2025 and reissued it in February 2026.

The 376 renumberings are the point. Text that did not change but moved would defeat a diff that matched on clause number, and would bury the 5 that genuinely changed.

Under the hood

Regulation becomes executable logic

None of the above works unless the rulebook underneath it is trustworthy. This is how a sentence in a PDF becomes something a machine can run and a person is accountable for.

01

Ingest

The PDF becomes a clause tree with stable ids. Reading the same file twice gives exactly the same result.

02

Propose

Deterministic extraction is the shipped path: all 1,377 rules came from the fixed extractor and no model was called. An LLM assisted escalation path exists for clauses the extractor cannot confidently reach.

03

Certify

A named officer settles anything the clause left open, then signs. The rule locks and cannot be edited after that.

04

Execute

Certified rules run against a firm's evidence and produce findings that cite the clause and the officer. No language model runs at this stage, ever.

05

Diff

When a circular is amended, the certifications that no longer cover the text they were signed over are named, one by one.

Probabilistic in the loop. Deterministic at the core. Human certified at the boundary.

A rule is drafted once and approved once. After that it runs the same way for every firm, every time, with no model in the path.

The decision everything rests on

Why is this not a chatbot?

The easy build is search plus a language model over the circulars. We chose not to build that. An answer you cannot sign, version or replay is not compliance, and a regulator will say so.

Ask a chatbot

You get an answer.

  • Different on a different day
  • Nothing you can sign or version
  • Nobody accountable for the reading
  • Cannot replay a decision made last year

Ask Sanhita

You get a position you can defend.

  • The requirement, and the clause it came from
  • Your evidence, and where in your document it was found
  • The assessment, with both input hashes
  • The gap, its owner and its deadline
  • The correction, and the re-check that accepted it
  • A hash chained record of all of it
